## Problem Statement

The app works. The data layer is baked and reproducible, the Empirical Bayes fit is
audited, 241 tests pass, and `app/streamlit_app.py` renders a map, a drawer, a ranked
table, a countermeasure estimator and a PDF export. What it does not do is make its own
finding legible to someone who arrives cold.

The audience for this session is **a portfolio reviewer who opens the deployed link with
zero context and gives it under a minute.** Against that audience the current first screen
fails in a specific, diagnosable way: nothing is selected on load, so the drawer, the
estimator and the export all render "select a corridor above" simultaneously. The reviewer
sees a map with no entry point and three empty boxes. A nine-bullet expander titled "What
each part of this page does" sits above it, which is the interface apologising in prose for
not explaining itself.

Meanwhile the strongest thing in the repo — that 861 of NYC's 1,945 traffic deaths sit in
crashes every other borough view drops — appears nowhere on the first screen.

## What Makes This Cool

The finding is not a data-cleaning footnote. Measured from `data/processed/crashes.parquet`
this session:

| Corridor | Crashes | Deaths | Deaths in the standard view |
|---|---|---|---|
| Belt Pkwy | 13,063 | 56 | **0** |
| Major Deegan Expy | 6,006 | 30 | 1 |
| Long Island Expy | 8,500 | 27 | **0** |
| Grand Central Pkwy | 8,526 | 23 | **0** |
| FDR Drive | 6,800 | 22 | **0** |
| Cross Bronx Expy | 6,322 | 20 | **0** |
| Brooklyn Queens Expy | 9,004 | 19 | **0** |
| Van Wyck Expy | 6,224 | 12 | **0** |

**Seven of New York City's major highways show zero traffic deaths in the standard
borough-level crash view. An eighth, the Major Deegan, shows one of its thirty.** Between
them those eight corridors have 209 deaths, rendered as 1. Across the twelve featured
corridors the standard view hides 225 of 288 deaths.

This is not an undercount, it is a systematic erasure of the highway network. And the rows
being erased are more lethal than the ones that survive: `scripts/verify_figures.py:62-64`
verifies the fatality rate in unlabeled rows at **3.191 per 1,000 against 1.872 in labeled
rows — 1.70×**. (That pair compares *borough-missing vs borough-present*, not highway vs
surface. Do not restate it as a highway figure; an earlier draft of this document did
exactly that.)

The reveal is demonstrable as a single interaction because `borough_source` is already a
three-way field in the committed Parquet: `reported` 578,929 · `recovered` 221,641 ·
`unrecoverable` 48,169.

Second, unexpected asset: **NYC's public crash feed has not advanced past 2026-06-11.**
Press reporting in September 2026 attributes this to a broken automated update process at
the Office of Technology and Innovation, whose projected August 2026 fix did not land. This
app's extract ends at exactly 2026-06-11 — the frontier of the public record — and
`app/live.py` already queries the Socrata API live to prove it. That button is currently two
clicks deep, behind a popover labelled "Why the lag? ▾".

**Constraint on how this reaches the UI.** The figures in that press report (54,832 logged
vs 36,195 published) are *not* computed by this repo, and "three months frozen" is precisely
the elapsed-days quantity `app/data.py:56-61` and `app/live.py` ban by name — it grows by one
every day and is wrong the moment a demo slips. So the freeze must reach the screen only as
`live.check_feed()` already reports it: two dates, both facts, neither rotting. The framing
changes ("the feed has not moved" is a stronger story than "the feed lags"); the numbers on
screen do not.

## Constraints

- **Portfolio piece, not a startup.** Success is a reviewer's impression, not retention.
- **Cold open, under a minute, no context, possibly zero clicks.**
- **No deployed URL exists.** Last commit (`6f3da79`) was fixing a Streamlit Cloud build
  failure. Deploy gates the *audience* (a cold reviewer needs a link) and the WCAG audit at
  `TODOS.md:32`, which is the one open item that genuinely depends on it — the other two P1s
  do not. Steps 2-4 below can proceed without a URL.
- `DESIGN.md` governs and wins over the spec. Map stays ≥65% of content width; completeness
  stays a neutral + hatch channel and never borrows a severity hue; `--expected` is reserved
  for EB figures; motion budget is drawer transition plus hover/focus only.
- No new runtime dependency. `requirements.txt` is what Community Cloud installs.
- **No figure is ever typed.** Every number rendered or published is computed from the
  Parquet. This rule caught the author of this very document mid-session (see Open Questions).

## Approaches Considered

**A — The Landing State.** Preselect a corridor, hoist the claim and live-feed button,
make the ranked table row-selectable and drive the map from it, delete the expander. No new
queries. Effort: human ~1 day / CC ~30 min. *Rejected as the primary: fixes the defects but
leaves the hook as a headline, so the app ends up competent and forgettable.*

**B — The Reveal.** One control re-renders map, drawer and table against reported-borough
rows only. Effort: human ~2-3 days / CC ~60-90 min. **Chosen — see below.**

**C — Split Screen.** Two synced maps, standard view vs. this tool, side by side, zero
clicks required. Effort: human ~3-4 days / CC ~2 hr. *Rejected for now: breaks DESIGN.md §3's
≥65% map-width rule, doubles WebGL cost on a Community Cloud container, and reads as
ambiguous without a caption. Revisit after B is deployed and seen cold.*

## Recommended Approach

**B — The Reveal.** Everything in A, plus one control directly beneath the headline claim:
*See what other tools show.* Flipping it re-renders the map, the drawer and the ranked table
against `borough_source = 'reported'` only. The highway network goes dark. Belt Pkwy falls
from rank 1 to 308 crashes and zero deaths.

In the reveal state the ranked table sorts by **observed crashes** (EB is unavailable there),
so Belt Pkwy drops from the top of the EB-ranked list to 308 observed crashes and zero
recorded deaths — beneath every surface street in the featured set, beneath four of the other
highways, and beneath roughly 450 corridors citywide. Rank and count are different axes; the
table header must name which one is in play in each state.

Chosen because it is the only option where the single thing a reviewer is most likely to
click is also the single thing they should remember, and it does so without breaking any
`DESIGN.md` rule.

Wireframe (two states, real figures): `docs/designs/wireframe-reveal.html`.

**Implementation notes.**

- `eb_cells.parquet` carries `lat_c, lon_c, canonical, observed, eb_estimate, eb_weight,
  is_highway, limited_access_share` — **no `borough_source`**. The existing map layer cannot
  do the reveal. It needs one new query aggregating `crashes_filtered` by `lat_c`/`lon_c`
  split on `borough_source`. The base Parquet carries all three columns and 848k rows in
  DuckDB is fast, so no re-bake is required.
- **Both map states must be driven by the same encoded variable.** The base map colours by
  `eb_estimate`; the reveal query returns observed crash counts. If the reveal swaps the
  encoded variable mid-comparison, the legend lies and the two states are not comparable.
  Drive *both* states from the new observed-count query and render the EB layer as a
  separate, clearly labelled mode — or keep EB as the default and make the reveal an overlay
  that dims unreported cells rather than recolouring everything.
- In the reveal state, EB figures must render as unavailable, not recomputed. The EB fit is
  over its own train/holdout window and has no reported-only variant. Showing a number there
  would be exactly the fabrication §4.2 exists to prevent. **Consequence:** in the reveal
  state the ranked table cannot sort by `eb_estimate` (which is what `corridor_table.sql`
  currently orders by). It must fall back to observed crashes, and say so in the column header.
- **Cache key.** `streamlit_app.py:255` calls `query(con, "cell_map", ("cell_map",))` with a
  constant key. The reveal flag must enter that tuple or the map serves a stale frame when
  toggled. Same for any new completeness query.
- **The reveal removes 1,657 corridors entirely, not just demotes them.** Only 7,274 of the
  8,931 canonicals have any `borough_source = 'reported'` row at all. If the selected
  corridor is one of the other 1,657, flipping the reveal empties the drawer — recreating
  criterion 2's empty-state failure inside the very interaction meant to fix it. **The reveal
  must keep the selected corridor's row present with explicit zeros**, never drop it. "0
  crashes, 0 deaths in the standard view" is the finding; a blank drawer is a bug.
- **The headline figure is computed once, unfiltered.** 861 / 1,945 / 44.3% is a property of
  the dataset, not of the current view. It must not be recomputed over `crashes_filtered`, or
  it would move when a reviewer touches the date picker or the casualty toggle — a hook that
  mutates under the reader is worse than a stale one. Compute at load from the full Parquet.
- **The dropdown needs a `key=` before any of this works.** `streamlit_app.py:215` declares
  the selectbox without one, so neither the `session_state` arbitration rule nor resetting it
  to "(none — city-wide)" is expressible until it has a key. Add it first.
- **Two selection sources need arbitration.** The dropdown yields a display label; the table
  yields `canonical`. Streamlit emits both on every rerun, so without a `session_state`
  last-touched rule they will fight. Specify: whichever widget changed this run wins, and
  `canonical` is the single stored value.
- **The display-label path breaks for 8,919 of 8,931 corridors.** `featured` has 12 rows, so
  a table row outside that set yields a `canonical` with no matching label. Today
  `streamlit_app.py:367` renders `### {selected_corridor}`, so a non-featured selection
  renders `### None`. The export failure is subtler and worse: `pdf_export.py:73` renders
  `corridor or 'City-wide (no corridor selected)'` and the filename falls back to
  `'citywide'`, so a corridor-scoped export would come out **headed "City-wide"** — a wrong
  document rather than an obviously broken one, which is precisely the §3.4 liability the
  export gate exists to prevent. The dropdown's display value **cannot** be derived for
  these 8,919. Specify a single resolver:
  `display = featured_label_for(canonical) or canonical`, used everywhere
  `selected_corridor` is read now, and reset the selectbox to "(none — city-wide)" whenever
  the table drives the selection.
- **Most corridors have no EB match, so table selection routinely degrades the page.**
  3,840 of 8,931 rows are `eb_matched = False`. Selecting one disables the estimator
  (`streamlit_app.py:503`) and blocks the export. That is correct behaviour per §4.2, but it
  will now be the *common* path rather than a rare one. Either sort/filter the table so
  matched corridors lead, or make the unmatched state read as an explained condition rather
  than a wall of disabled controls.
- **Motion.** `DESIGN.md` §6 budgets drawer transition plus hover/focus only. Selecting a
  corridor should highlight it and recentre the viewport **without** an animated fly-to.
- The copy in the "Why the lag?" popover currently attributes the gap to the police-reporting
  pipeline. That is true in general but is **not** the current cause; the current cause is a
  broken ETL job at the Office of Technology and Innovation. Rewrite it to say both.

2. On first paint, no element renders an empty "select a corridor above" state.
3. The completeness claim is visible above the fold without scrolling, at 1280px and at 820px.

6. The live-feed button is reachable in one click from the first screen.
7. The nine-bullet expander is deleted, and nothing on the page describes behaviour the code

8. `pytest -q` stays green. Note the gap: `tests/test_live.py`'s banned-words grep covers
